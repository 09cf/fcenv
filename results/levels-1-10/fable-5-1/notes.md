FANTASTIC CONTRAPTION NOTES (carry over between levels)

== Core mechanics (confirmed) ==
- +y is DOWN. Angles clockwise, 0=right, 90=down.
- cw wheel on ground drives RIGHT; ccw drives LEFT (regardless of how the car is flipped: rotation sense is global). cw wheel against a CEILING drives left, ccw against ceiling drives right. Wheel dia 40, ~0.8 turns/s = 5 rad/s => ~100 units/s top speed. Motor acts relative to what the centre is joined to (lone wheel does nothing). Velocity motor: BRAKES if spun faster than target.
- Motor torque strong: cw wheel on fixed frame lifted the goal ball hung 60 below over the top in ~1 s; swung goal RECT rigidly hung 70 below (3 rim rods) over the top in 2.6 s (L8). Could NOT lift ball at r=115 on heavy 6-rod arm. Wheel push force at ground = T/20 => pushing is TRACTION/weight limited => make pushing cars HEAVY (many wheels). A pushed car whose wheels spin the opposite way resists with mu*W: pusher must be >2x heavier (3x worked, L9).
- WHEEL FRICTION IS HIGH (mu >= ~1): cw wheels pressed against a near-vertical convex face (circle r=102) CLIMB it, and a whole 6-wheel truss car hung on the face by friction alone (L10). Wall/hump climbing with cw wheels (wall on the right) is a real technique.
- Wheel rim joints rotate with the wheel: slots 1 right, 2 bottom, 3 left, 4 top. cw wheel carries a rim arm DOWN -> LEFT -> UP -> RIGHT. Rods joined to a rim joint may overlap the wheel. Rigid rect-on-wheel: rim2->0:0, rim3->0:0, rim3->rect corner 0:2.
- Gravity 300 u/s^2, 30 ticks/s. Joints are pins; NEVER seen one break: a 2-wheel+ball triangle car dropped 274 units (~400 u/s landing) survived intact and drove on (L9); a tether chain yanked taut at ~200 u/s held.
- Wood rod: 8 thick, collides with everything except water rods. Water rod: 4 thick, collides only with terrain AND level blocks; passes through own pieces/goal pieces. Water rods MAY cross wood rods / pass near ball at BUILD time too (accepted). Chain of water rods = a rope that wraps terrain corners (stiff links; use short links). Rope mass = 4*length (200-long rope ~ 65% of ball mass) -> drags/slows a rolling ball.
- Pieces sharing a joint never collide with each other -- EXCEPT: a wheel centred on a goal-rect corner (0:1) DOES collide with the rect: the build is accepted but at run time the overlap explodes, twisting the truss within 5 ticks (L10). Never put a wheel on a goal-piece joint. (Rods joined to rect corners that run along/near the rect edge are fine.)
- All pieces similar density (mass ~ area): ball/wheel area 1257 ~ 4 rods of length 40; goal rect 84x70 ~ 4.7 wheel masses (a big chunk of a car's mass: its position sets the COM). Heavy masses NOT buildable -> trebuchets/launchers infeasible (ball max ~300 u/s). Rolling ball on ramp: a = g*sin(theta)/1.5 ideal, real less.
- Goal piece joints: circle centre "0:0" (rim 0:1 right, 0:2 bottom, 0:3 left, 0:4 top); rect 0=centre, 0:1 TR, 0:2 TL, 0:3 BR, 0:4 BL. move_joint on 0:0 repositions the goal piece (must stay in build area incl radius). Goal pieces are rigid truss nodes (rect as top chord member held under hump-climbing loads, L10). Move the goal piece BEFORE adding rods to it. A goal ball as a free-spinning vertex of a car acts as a roller/foot.
- Win = all goal pieces fully inside goal area at one moment (flying through counts; a tilted rect on a nose-down car counted, L10). Submit re-runs deterministically: a solved run submits fine even with 2 units of margin. Tumbling/landing is deterministic too -> empirical tuning is valid.
- Level dynamic blocks are heavy (~1 wheel mass each) and movable; pushable off edges to fill gaps; dynamic circle r=82 rolled easily when pushed at its equator. Level pieces never connectable. Restitution is low (landings don't bounce much).

== Build rules / gotchas ==
- Everything inside build area incl. thickness: wheel centres >= 20 from edges, rod ends >= 4.
- Ground clearance: wheel centre > 20 above terrain top (ground 211.9 -> wheel y=190 ok); rod end >= 4 above. Circle terrain: surface y = cy - sqrt(r^2-(x-cx)^2). Level dynamic circles sitting on a platform block wheel placement (keep wheel centre >= R+20 away).
- Overlap rules: pieces may overlap ONLY if they share a joint. Crossing wood rods = rejected. Rod within 24 of a wheel centre it isn't joined to = rejected (24.8 accepted; measure to the SEGMENT, endpoints count) -- this bites when a ballast wheel sits on a top node next to a diagonal: compute cross products before building; rod within ~8 of a rod it isn't joined to = rejected; rod within 24 of goal ball centre = rejected unless joined to 0:0 (25 ok). No duplicate rods. A joint holds at most ONE wheel. A free [x,y] point always makes a NEW joint.
- Terrain-overlap check includes wheel radius. build is all-or-none; "@i:s" refs pieces in the same call (rod ends 0/1, wheel centre 0, rim 1-4). Rejection lists every overlap.
- Rods up to ~270 long fine. Wheel spacing >= 43-45 between centres.
- Static frame on ground needs >= 2 ground nodes rigidly connected; always triangulate. Check 24-unit wheel clearance BEFORE building.
- Floating build area: everything drops when run; wheels all at same y.

== Workflow ==
describe + view("level") -> geometry on paper -> one build call -> save -> run(600) -> run_filmstrip/run_trails/run_frame -> notes -> submit. If stuck, run CHEAP PHYSICS EXPERIMENTS first (drop test, torque test, "just drive at it"): L9 drop test and L10 "drive the truss at the hump" each showed the real behaviour in one run. Simple heavy robust cars solve most things; static frame + wheel-arm solved L8. Think about what the puzzle REALLY needs (energy, direction change, reach, COM position) before building clever stuff. When a run looks weird (car tilted, creeping), zoom a frame + stride-5 filmstrip over the first 40 ticks: structural failures show up in the first ticks.

== Designs ==
- Triangle car (3 rods + 2 wheels + goal ball apex): climbs ~27 deg; survives huge drops; lands on any side with a wheel touching -> always drives.
- Gap bridger: 4+ wheels in a row, span > gap, all cw, triangulated. Tipping: COM must be well behind the near edge.
- LONG WARREN-TRUSS CAR (L7): 11 cw wheels spacing 45, bottom chord, top nodes 45 above at bay midpoints, diagonals, top chord; goal rect on top. Bridged 175 gap.
- WARREN-TRUSS BULLDOZER (L5): 6 cw wheels + chords, top nodes 47 above, diagonals, 3 unpowered ballast wheels on rear top nodes, tall blade + braces.
- SMALL MAST DOZER (L6): 3 cw wheels, 2 top nodes, ballast wheel, bumper at obstacle equator height; goal ball on 3-rod mast.
- SNOWPLOW / CONVEYOR CAR (L4): ground cw wheels + ramp row of CCW wheels; never a bare cw wheel at the very front.
- WHEEL-ARM LIFTER (L8): static triangulated tower, cw wheel at top, goal rect hung rigidly 70 below via rim rods; swings it up into a goal band above. Arm radius <= ~70.
- PUSHER + CCW DROP CAR (L9): goal under the platform overhang -> heavy cw pusher shoves a light 2-ccw-wheel + ball triangle car off the edge; it drops 274, lands, drives left. Direction reversal = opposite-spin car delivered by gravity.
- HUMP CLIMBER (L10 "up the hump", tick 267): ground with a circular hump r=102 (top 100 above ground, near-vertical sides), goal beyond it. 6-wheel Warren truss (wheels x -98..120 spacing 43.6, y=190; top nodes 49 above at bay midpoints) with the GOAL RECT AT THE FRONT as the top-chord member (bottom corners 0:4,0:3 = front top nodes; diagonals W3->0:4, W4->0:4, W4->0:3, W5->0:3) plus two unpowered ballast wheels stacked at the front (node B=(120,141) joined to W5,W6,0:3; node C=(120,95) joined to B,0:1,0:3). Front-heavy (COM ~60% from the rear). Sequence: front wheels climb the face (ticks 90-150), whole car lifted onto the hump by friction, tips FORWARD over the crest (~tick 210), slides nose-down (~40 deg) the far side; rect inside goal at tick 267 while still descending. Same truss with REAR ballast climbed the face but wheelied backwards off the hump (COM behind the contact point) and got stuck. Rule: when a rigid car must go over a convex obstacle, COM must be ahead of the contact point once lifted.
- TETHER/ROPE (tried L9, nearly worked): water-rod chain anchored near a corner lets a ball swing around under an overhang. Needs horizontal speed when the rope goes taut.
- Ideas not yet needed: water braces inside an arm's sweep; unpowered wheels as sliders; cw wheel against wall on its right climbs it (ccw: wall on left) -- CONFIRMED L10; straddling frames; push round things with a rod bumper at centre height; hinged boom that falls over the edge and rests on the corner = pivot outside the build area.
